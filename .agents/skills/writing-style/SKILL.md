# Writing Style

Use this skill when drafting or revising engineer-facing documentation. Optimize for a scanning reader who needs to understand the problem, the proposed solution, and the decisions or work required.

## Core style

- Lead with the point, then provide the details needed to act.
- Be concise, clear, concrete, and direct.
- Prefer simple English over unnecessary technical jargon.
- Replace vague terms with specific nouns, actions, states, file paths, APIs, or workflows.
- Use examples when they remove ambiguity.
- Surface important constraints, limitations, risks, and open questions early.
- Remove duplicated ideas and repeated phrasing.

## Structure

Choose the structure that best fits the document, but for design and plan documents usually cover:

1. Goal.
2. Current problem or constraint.
3. Proposed approach.
4. Limitations, risks, and open questions.
5. Concrete examples, paths, APIs, states, or workflows.

Use headings, bullets, and ordering to make the document easy to scan. Do not force this sequence when another structure makes the subject clearer.

## Final review

Before finishing, check:

- Can a scanning reader identify the problem and proposed solution in under a minute?
- Are important limitations visible before implementation details?
- Are ambiguous words replaced with concrete nouns and actions?
- Have duplicated ideas and repeated phrasing been removed?
- Does every section help the reader make a decision or do the work?
